**How Social Media Use Affects Adolescent Brain Development** (URL: [https://healthmatters.nyp.org/how-social-media-use-affects-adolescent-brain-development/](https://healthmatters.nyp.org/how-social-media-use-affects-adolescent-brain-development/))  
*(Published May 2023\)*

* Summary of the JAMA study results ([https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/2799812](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/2799812))   
  * Study found that **kids who habitually check social media had changes in parts of the brain that control social rewards and punishments**   
* “These platforms give adolescents the opportunity for **increased social interaction at a time when their brains are especially sensitive to social feedback, particularly reward feedback**. Over the course of three years, the researchers looked at the brain activity of teens who habitually checked social media against those who didn’t and found changes in the areas of the brain associated with social rewards and punishments. It’s **too early to say if social media was the only cause of these changes, but it does suggest that more studies are needed** to better understand brain development in the age of social media.”   
  – Dr. Heidi Allison Bender (neuropsychologist w/ NewYork-Presbyterian/Weill Cornell Medical Center)  
* Different areas of the brain impacted:  
  * Amygdala (one of the emotional centers of the brain) preferentially affected  
  * Dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (responsible for judgement, reasoning and rewards)  
  * Other brain areas involved in processing positive or negative emotions showed lower sensitivity to social anticipation in those who checked more frequently than those who did not  
* Long term changes in brain observed but not enough info to make definitive conclusions  
  * “Is it necessarily a bad thing to have more sensitivity to social cues? Maybe it will make someone more emotionally adept. These changes in brain development don’t necessarily have to be negative.”   
* Could spiral into an elevated need for frequent stimulation to prompt “dopamine dump” or release  
  * Kids could spend time looking for next reward from social media instead of experiencing joy in real life  
* **Over 50% of kids aged 4 or younger are engaging with smartphones and tablets on a regular basis; tween group (ages 8-12) spend four to six hours on phones or watching screens daily** *(from 2020 study)* ([https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2020/07/28/childrens-engagement-with-digital-devices-screen-time/](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2020/07/28/childrens-engagement-with-digital-devices-screen-time/))   
* Increased screen time can **reduce integrity of brain structures that support early literacy in pre-kindergarten students**  
* American Academy of Pediatrics and the American Academy of Adolescent Psychiatry **recommend no screen time until kids are 18 to 24 months old, and then gradually introducing screen time as they get older** ([https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families\_and\_Youth/Facts\_for\_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-And-Watching-TV-054.aspx](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-And-Watching-TV-054.aspx))   
* Pediatricians recommend open dialogue between parents/caregivers and children about the positives and negatives of social media
