**Examining the Relationship Between High School Students’ Fear of Missing Out (FoMO) on Social Media and Their Academic Procrastination Behaviors** (URL: [https://www.jppci.com/files/65/PUB/MA/69ABF10377A8E/M8904-193447.pdf](https://www.jppci.com/files/65/PUB/MA/69ABF10377A8E/M8904-193447.pdf))  
*Published 19 Mar 2026*

- Drawing on previous research, authors state that “academic procrastination is characterized by the voluntary postponement of academic duties and is consistently associated with reduced student achievement”  
- Study (note: correlational) aims to examine association between social media-induced FoMO and procrastination in high school student population (while factoring in potential impact of demographic-based differences)  
    - Population is 450 students across different high schools in Çanakkale city center, Turkey  
- Collection methods: online demographic form, Academic Procrastination Scale, and social media-focused FoMO scale

- Amongst the 450 high school students, the average score on the Social Media Fear of MIssing Out (FoMO) scale was 20.57, from a range of 9 to 63 where higher scores indicate relatively greater tendencies for FoMO  
    - Therefore, important to note that **high school students in this cohort had relatively low propensity for FoMO related to SMU to begin with**; the potential association between this variable and academic procrastination applies only insofar as students actually experience FoMO (and this shows why experience of SMU and impacts can be very different for different individuals)  
- **FoMO levels were found to vary moderately based on gender (22.44 points on scale for female students vs. 18.82 points on scale for male students)**, have no significant variance by grade level, and vary significantly by type of high school (Turkey designations; ex.: Anatolian High Schools, Social Sciences High Schools, etc.)  
- After evaluating procrastination tendencies amongst the cohort independently of FoMO using the Academic Procrastination Scale (self-report), which found a moderate prevalence of academic procrastination (55.31 points on scale, ranging from 19 to 95), Pearson analysis yielded a **statistically significant yet weak positive link between FoMO and academic procrastination levels in the population**  
    - **“approximately 5% common variance”** (11)  
    - Authors’ analysis: “Although the strength of this association is modest, it nonetheless indicates that **FoMO functions as a meaningful, albeit limited, risk factor within adolescents’ academic self-regulation processes**” (14)  
        - **Suggests** that **“as students’ anxiety regarding missed social opportunities intensifies, their likelihood of engaging in academic procrastination similarly increases”** (14)  
            - Given the association between social media and FoMO found in other scientific literature, shows potential for social media overuse to impact academic performance via increased procrastination tendencies  
- **Important to interpret impact of FoMO on academic behavior “as part of a broader network of internal self-regulatory mechanisms and external contextual influences that jointly shape adolescents’ academic functioning”** (14)  
    - Correlative study is very much limited in that it does not factor in many of these aspects; it merely suggests a potential, partial relationship in theory
