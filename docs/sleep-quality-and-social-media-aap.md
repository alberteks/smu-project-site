* National Sleep Foundation (NSF) recommends that children ages 6-13 get 9-11 hours of sleep per night, and adolescents 14-17 years of age get 8-10 hours per night  
    * Source: [https://www.thensf.org/how-many-hours-of-sleep-do-you-really-need/](https://www.thensf.org/how-many-hours-of-sleep-do-you-really-need/)   
* Risks associated with not getting sufficient sleep for children & adolescents:  
    * Trouble thinking clearly  
    * Poor academic performance  
        * Source: [https://www.sleephealthjournal.org/article/S2352-7218(15)00160-6/fulltext](https://www.sleephealthjournal.org/article/S2352-7218\(15\)00160-6/fulltext)   
    * Feeling less awake or aware  
    * Depressed mood  
    * Anxiety symptoms  
    * Obesity  
* **“Therefore, anything that gets in the way of sleep, including smart phones, can be a risk.”**  
* B-SATED (Behavior, Satisfaction, Alertness, Timing, Efficiency, and Duration) \= comprehensive model designed to optimize sleep health for youth adolescents by addressing six dimensions (Source: [https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1087079221000101?via%3Dihub](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1087079221000101?via%3Dihub))   
    * Sleep-related **behaviors** include factors that promote sleep (e.g., consistent sleep schedule, bedtime routine) & those that could **negatively impact sleep (e.g., caffeine and technology use in the hour before bed)**  
    * **Satisfaction** with sleep is a subjective feeling of whether the person thought their sleep was good/bad in quality. Phones and other electronic devices can play a role in this **(if phones are keeping people awake and distracting them, they may report less satisfaction with sleep quality)**  
    * **Alertness** means the ability to remain alert and awake during the day. **Cross-sectional study of 4153 U.S. adolescents ages 12-17 years found that adolescents who posted on or checked social media more frequently and those who perceived social media to be more important for social belonging had higher levels of daytime sleepiness**   
        * Source: [https://mental.jmir.org/2021/9/e26273](https://mental.jmir.org/2021/9/e26273)   
    * **Timing** of sleep refers to when a person goes to bed and wakes up with a regular routine aligning with natural sleep cycles showing best results. A **review article found several studies that suggested adolescents may postpone bedtime so they can continue using devices for entertainment (behavioral bedtime delay).** When adolescents postpone sleep, they may not be able to also postpone wake-up time, leading to shorter sleep  
        * Source: [https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1056499317301384?via%3Dihub](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1056499317301384?via%3Dihub)   
    * Sleep **efficiency** refers to how easy it is for one to fall asleep & return to sleep if they wake in the middle of the night. **Phone alerts may interfere with sleep efficiency**  
    * Sleep duration refers to the total amount of sleep a person gets per day. Number of recommended hours an individual should get changes across development  
* Tips for promoting healthy adolescent sleep habits:  
    * Consider putting phone on charger somewhere where you can’t reach from bed to avoid waking up throughout night to check phone  
        * Can also use phone settings to silence notifications at night  
    * Work to build bedtime routine that doesn’t include social media in hours leading up to bedtime  
        * E.g., showering, choosing outfit for next day, reading, journaling, listening to podcast, doing art  
        * Build in time for social media earlier in evening  
    * Participate in physical activity along with healthy eating to remain alert and awake during day  
    * Aim for recommended amount of sleep each night based on age & development  
        * Try to keep routine consistent each night to coincide with natural sleep-wake rhythms
