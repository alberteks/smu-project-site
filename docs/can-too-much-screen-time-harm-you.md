Summary: *Excessive screen time (prolonged use of phones, tablets and similar devices) common among teens (reported at about 7 hours 22 minutes per day in 2019\) and can affect adolescent brain development (some heavy users showed thinner cortical regions), making teens especially vulnerable during this critical neurodevelopmental stage. It also disrupts sleep (blue light lowers melatonin), weakens immunity and learning, and is linked to depression and anxiety; practical steps include using apps to set limits, switching devices off 30–60 minutes before bed, scheduling screen-free activities and exercise, and talking with a trusted adult if overwhelmed.*

* Definition: Excessive screen time involves prolonged engagement with electronic devices like phones, tablets, and even watches  
* Teens reportedly spend an average of **seven hours and 22 minutes on screens every day, not including computer time for schoolwork** ([https://www.commonsensemedia.org/kids-action/articles/tweens-teens-and-phones-what-our-2019-research-reveals](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/kids-action/articles/tweens-teens-and-phones-what-our-2019-research-reveals))   
  * Note: this stat is apparently from 2019 so I’ll be on the lookout for a more up-to-date finding on this  
* Studies indicate that too much screen time can negatively affect the body  
  * Adolescence is a critical time for brain development, making teenagers particularly vulnerable to the effects of excessive screen time  
  * **Too much screen time may cause physical changes to the brain, affecting the growth of the cortex** (outer layer of the brain that processes information)  
    * A study called Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) revealed that **some kids who use screens more than seven hours a day had a thinner cortex than those who used screens less** ([https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1878929317301214](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1878929317301214))  
* Excessive screen time can disrupt sleep  
  * Staying up late interacting with devices contributes to this disruption  
  * **Bright screen lights & blue light reduce melatonin** (darkness hormone that plays a big role in sleepiness) **production**  
  * Not getting sufficient sleep weakens the immune system and reduces ability to concentrate, learn, and remember/recall information  
* Excessive screen time has been linked to depression and other mental health disorders  
  * Too much screen time may lead teens to **neglect responsibilities, use screens to deal with stress, and feel anxious without a device**  
* Tips for “turning off”  
  * Use app to track screen time and set limits  
  * Switch off all devices 30-60 minutes before bed  
  * Schedule time for screen-free activities (going outside, hobbies, hanging out with friends, etc.)  
  * Exercise   
  * Keep a journal and/or talk to a trusted adult (parent, counselor, doctor, etc.) if overwhelmed
