**Social Media Use and Academic Achievement**   
(URL: [https://www.udel.edu/udaily/2024/march/social-media-academic-achievement-research/](https://www.udel.edu/udaily/2024/march/social-media-academic-achievement-research/))

*Published March 2024*

* “UD Associate Professor Mellissa Gordon finds that frequent social media use is associated with decreased academic achievement among early adolescents”  
* In an article, Gordon & co-author Ohannessian analyzed survey data from 1459 middle schoolers in the northeast U.S. and **found that their academic achievement decreased as their Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, and Twitter/X use increased**  
    * **Parental communication made a big difference, playing a part in whether school grades increased or decreased**  
    * Study focused exclusively on early adolescents (11 to 15 y/os) – the fastest growing population of social media users – and assessed 4 social media platforms  
    * Analyzed self-reported data on their school grades & SMU from surveys given in the fall of 2016 and spring of 2017  
    * Even after controlling for age, gender, and race/ethnicity, they found that participants’ grades decreased as the frequency of their SMU increased across all 4 platforms   
* “There are many explanations for this finding, which aligns with research on older population groups as well,” Gordon writes  
    * “For example, social media likely poses a distraction to early adolescents. Attention that they would typically invest in their schoolwork is diverted to social media use, which ultimately affects their ability to perform well in school. But lower academic achievement may also result from other aspects of development that are affected by social media use. For example, social media use can disrupt healthy family functioning or peer relationships, which can then lower early adolescents’ performance in school.”  
* Role of parental communication  
    * In the study, they asked participants to define the quality of their communication w/ their mothers (e.g., agree or disagree w/ a series of 20 statements, such as “My mother is always a good listener”)  
    * Found that less frequent use of Facebook and Instagram, coupled w/ high-quality mother-adolescent communication, was associated with higher academic achievement   
        * “We think that mothers who were maintaining positive, frequent communication with their children might have also been monitoring their adolescents’ use of Facebook and Instagram, perhaps by setting daily limits,” Gordon said  
    * Low quality mother-adolescent communication and increased use of Facebook and Instagram was associated with lower academic achievement  
        * Gordon and Ohannessian suggest that frequent SMU may allow an adolescent to establish more autonomy from their parents, which is a developmentally appropriate behavior for this age group – however communication with their mothers and the monitoring of social media may decrease
