**Prevalence of Eating Disorders and Their Association with Social Media Addiction among Youths** (URL: [https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/15/21/4687](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/15/21/4687)) 

*(Published Nov 2023\)*

* 350 participants aged 14-25 from Pakistan–note that the study’s authors specifically want to explore “the connection between eating disorders among adolescent boys and girls and social media … in developing countries,” so although some takeaways will be similar to US experience may vary due to socioeconomic factors as well  
* Authors define SM addiction as “so involved in social media that they are distressed when they cannot use it”  
* Researchers used two pre-validated survey methods to collect data: Eating Attitude Test (EAT-26) and Social Networking Addiction Scale (SNAS)  
* Authors note that “**after substance abuse, eating disorders rank second in the mortality rate of any mental health issue**,” showing that a potential association between social media addiction and eating disorders would present a significant modern issue 

* **Almost 38% of participants reported daily Internet use of 4-6 hrs**  
* **48.9% of youths used social media for 1-3 hrs daily**, and **32.3% spent 4-6 hrs daily**  
* **41.7% of the participants** (and just under 60% of female participants) were found via EAT-26 to **have a likely chance of developing eating disorders**. Similarly **42% overall** and 57.8% of female participants **were labeled as having social media addiction** based on the SNAS  
* “The tendency towards social media addiction significantly differed based on age … **Youths aged 18-21 years had higher scores for social media addiction**”   
  * Shows that younger populations may be more susceptible to excessive social media use, or at the very least use it far more frequently than older populations and therefore have a greater amount impacted by potential negative effects  
* Associating results of EAT-26 and SNAS for participants, **social media had weak positive relationship** w/ overall eating disorders, particularly **with bulimia and food preoccupation**   
    
* Note that, as other self-report study methodologies, this may be susceptible to response bias so other studies on this topic would be useful
