*(Last Updated: February 2024\)*

* There’s not much research on whether social media time limits work, but some early evidence gives us hints – In one study, college students were put into one of three groups:  
  * Control group – could use social media as they normally do  
  * Limited use group – could use social media for only 30 minutes a day  
  * Limited but active use group – could use social media for only 30 minutes a day but had to do something active on social media every 3 minutes (like posting or commenting)  
* Results:  
  * **Limited use group w/ a 30-min time limit had less depression symptoms than the control group**  
    * However, **students in limited use group who described themselves as highly active on social media reported more loneliness, anxiety, & lower self-esteem**  
    * Suggests that for those who don’t post or comment as often or use social media to browse, limiting their use of social media might reduce feelings of sadness (however, setting limits might be more harmful for those who are naturally active on it)  
  * **Students who were naturally more passive on social media & were in the limited but active group also showed lower well-being**  
    * Forcing less-active people to use social media in more active ways doesn’t seem to help & may actually increase negative symptoms  
* Time limits should ideally not be longer than you might naturally have used the platform, as this may encourage using them longer than planned  
* Simply restricting SMU doesn’t prevent problematic internet use (using social media impulsively or in risky ways)  
* Key takeaways & tips:  
  * Be easy on yourself   
    * Acknowledge managing social media is challenging and takes time & practice  
  * Shift focus to how and why you use social media  
    * Instead of tracking time, it may be effective to think about when, where, how, and why you use social media  
    * For instance, think about avoiding social media at bedtime, which has been shown to disrupt sleep. Instead build in time during the day for important activities on social media that can prevent you from using it at night  
  * Be able to explain why and how you use social media  
  * Develop a plan w/ parents/caregivers  
  * It’s (more than) okay to ask for help  
    * If social media is having a negative effect on your life, *please* reach out to trusted adults for help\!  
    * If you feel comfortable, talk to your parents/guardians about social media & what you’re struggling with  
      * E.g., ask them to talk about how they manage their use or what they do when they run into a tricky situation. They might help you think critically about what to do & how to protect yourself and your well-being
