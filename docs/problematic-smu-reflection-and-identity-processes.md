**Exploring the Role of Problematic Social Network Site Use in the Link Between Reflective Functioning and Identity Processes in Adolescents** (URL: [https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11469-022-00800-6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11469-022-00800-6)) 

*(Published Mar 2022\)*

* Authors note that SNS satisfies adolescents’ desire for social interaction & comparison and is therefore widespread, but that there are significant risks associated w/ negative social comparison  
* Study aims to explore specific relationship between RF (reflective functioning), identity development, and PSNSU (problematic social networking sites use)   
    * “Reflective functioning (**RF**) **is** defined as **the capacity to understand and interpret one’s own and others’ behaviour as an expression of mental states** such as feelings, thoughts, fantasies, beliefs, and desires (Fonagy et al., 2018)”   
    * Authors cite variety of studies showing associations between deficiencies in RF and other behavioral addictions in adolescence (including gambling and problematic–general–mobile phone use)   
        * For PSNSU specifically, presented **hypothesis that individuals w/ lower RF may find it easier to maintain interpersonal relationships online due to immediate feedback, but could then experience PSNSU as a result of “excessive reliance on SNS relationships**”   
            * Potential takeaway **if** such a conclusion is **supported: SMU can become problematic if relied on too heavily due to perception of easier social interaction**

* Sample of 354 high school students in Italy (ages 13-19)  
* Students filled out anonymous questionnaire assessing RF, SNSU, and identity development  
    * RF was measured through Reflective Functioning Scale (RFQ-8) w/ 8 questions on a scale from 1 to 7 (strongly disagree to strongly agree, respectively)   
        * Measured “to what extent participants are able to understand the self and others in terms of internal mental status”   
    * PSNSU was measured through Italian version of Internet Addiction Test (IAT)   
        * 20 items on 5-point scale “measuring the extent to which participants reported problematic involvement with SNS,” such as staying on SNS for longer than originally intended  
    * Identity was measured through Italian version of Utrecht-Management of Identity Commitments Scale (U-MICS), including identity commitment, in-depth exploration, & reconsideration of commitment. Was conducted twice for each of identity in friendship relationships and school  
        * Researchers specifically focused on these domains because they identified them as especially relevant for the adolescent age group  
        
* Reporting on Internet use in the Italian IAT, “**participants** declared they **spend most of their time online on social media …, watching movies …, and only a small portion of their time playing video games**”   
    * Supports idea that SMU is one of the most common online activities for adolescents in modern world   
* **Hours a day spent online**, logically, “**positively related with PSNSU**” **and** also “**weakly and negatively related with identity commitment**”   
* In line with the researchers’ hypotheses, “**RF negatively associated with PSNSU and reconsideration of commitment and positively associated with identity commitment**”   
    * If, as researchers suggested, RF abilities enable people to seek out relationships easier in-person and impaired RF abilities lead some to seek out relationships online due to more immediate feedback, this association makes a lot of sense   
* **PSNSU** was **“negatively related with identity commitment and in-depth exploration, while positively related with reconsideration of commitment**”   
    
* Authors concluded: “results … showed that PSNSU fully mediated the relationship between RF and identity in-depth exploration, and it partially mediated the relationship between RF and reconsideration of commitment”   
    * “**Adolescents with deficiencies in RF tended to use SNS more problematically**, and, **in turn, PSNSU was negatively related with identity in-depth exploration and positively related with reconsideration of commitment**”   
        * Shows that **adolescents finding difficulty building in-person relationships may turn to SMU, but heavy reliance on SMU leads to difficulty** in **exploring & committing to well-developed personal and social identity**  
            * Potentially due to immediate & (sometimes) negative comparisons abundant in SM interactions
