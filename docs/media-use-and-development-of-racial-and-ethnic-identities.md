**Media Use and the Development of Racial and Ethnic Identities** (URL: [https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/9781119011071.iemp0083](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/9781119011071.iemp0083))  
*(Published June 2020\)*

* Racial/ethnic media portrayals influence identity development through many psychological processes, including categorization & social comparison  
  * Implicit or explicit  
* Concept of social identity gratifications (SIG) arose out of the integration of social identity theory & uses gratifications theory in order to explain how social group identification was related to media use & perceptions  
  * Social identity theory proposes that our personal identities are made up of social group affiliations (such as race and ethnicity) and that we strive to maintain positive social group identities in part to preserve a positive self-concept  
  * Uses and gratifications theory proposes that media use is active and purposeful to meet our needs and desires  
* A person who more strongly identifies w/ their racial/ethnic group is likely to gravitate toward and select media featuring people of the same racial or ethnic identity  
  * Immersion w/ others of the same racial identity allows one’s own racial identity to form more solidly  
  * This would result, in media contexts, in a selective exposure to media representations similar to the self  
* SIG suggests that media use can reinforce identity  
  * When representations of race or ethnicity are problematic, however, they can be damaging to social identity  
    * E.g., stereotypic media portrayals of underrepresented minority groups  
  * Lack of media representation of certain groups can serve as a signal that society values some social groups less than others   
* Social media, in particular, merge interpersonal and mediated experiences  
  * We use it to maintain, develop, & dissolve relationships both w/ people we know offline and people we met through the internet  
  * Microaggressions directed toward underrepresented minorities are known to occur via social media  
  * Hate speech is also prevalent on social media  
* Social comparison theory: some people may be more strongly oriented to engage in social comparison processes  
  * **In research study, college students w/ a higher social comparison orientation were more likely to use social media heavily than their peers w/ lower social comparison orientation, and they had lower self-esteem and more negative self-perceptions**  
  * **Overall, identification w/ a media model of the same race/ethnicity can serve to either boost or weaken self-concept, depending on the nature of the media portrayal & our assessment of how we stack up to the mediated model**  
* Media may highlight the contrast (differences) between racial/ethnic groups  
  * In the media industry, the reliance on (over)generalizations of social groups is often thought to help the media user make sense of the information  
  * This may reinforce “us versus them” mentality that drives privileging of one’s own identity
