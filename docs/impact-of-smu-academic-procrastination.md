**Impact of Social Media Usage on Academic Procrastination** (URL: [http://dx.doi.org/10.31703/gesr.2022(VII-II).24](http://dx.doi.org/10.31703/gesr.2022\(VII-II\).24))   
*(Published 2022\)*

- Study “was designed and conducted to assess the association between social media usage with academic procrastination in undergraduate, graduate, and post-graduate students of Peshawar University”  
    - Researchers were motivated by concerns from teachers and parents about students’ potential inability to perform well on academic tasks due to some spending “excessive time” on social networking sites  
    - Note that **previous literature has been conflicting regarding the general associations between social media use and academic performance; some found lower GPAs and worse exam performances among heavy SNS users, while others “support\[ed\] that SNSs help students in their learning** by encouraging discussion, knowledge-sharing, interaction with teacher/supervisor in an informal setting, and easy access to information”  
- Population was 240 students across ten departments of Peshawar University, evenly split by gender  
- Measured social media use via Social Networking Time Use Scale (SONTUS) developed in previous study–Olufadi (2016)  
    - Scale measures time spent on different SNS across different situations/contexts for week; subscale scores then compound to global score which indicates “low, average, high and extremely high” use levels  
- Measured academic procrastination levels via Academic Procrastination Scale (APS) developed in McCloskey 2011  
- Finally, demographic data sheet was administered and included “brief questions regarding the respondents’ social media exposure and use”   
- Note that **study is correlative; does not explain relationship between variables** and may be missing factors, it simply identifies whether some statistically significant association exists

- **Majority of respondents** (60%) was **found to have “average” use level of SNSs**  
- Similarly, most students (68.3%) were found to have “average” academic procrastination levels  
- In general, **“both genders engaged more in online media than in enrichment activities”** based on initial demographic survey; these “enriching hobbies” included reading, writing, computer programming, and blogging  
- Study **found** a statistically significant **positive correlation (r=.424) between respondents’ social networking time use scores and academic procrastination scores**  
    - There was also an **inverse relationship between the SONTUS/APS scores, and educational level/academic scores**  
- In terms of usage patterns, study found that **younger students used social media websites more frequently** than older students  
- Authors conclude that “**students who excessively use social media are at a greater risk of indulging in academic procrastination**. Also, academic procrastination is adversely related to academic performance.”
