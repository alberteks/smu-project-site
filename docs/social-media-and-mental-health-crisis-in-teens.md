**Social Media and Mental Health in Children and Teens**  
(URL: [https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/wellness-and-prevention/social-media-and-mental-health-in-children-and-teens](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/wellness-and-prevention/social-media-and-mental-health-in-children-and-teens))

* Social media can be beneficial for teens’ mental health   
  * Provides access to information that might be unavailable elsewhere  
  * Can be a safe place to express oneself  
  * Breaking barriers such as distance and shyness in approaching others  
* Many teens can find support and seek help, as social media may help:  
  * Initiate medical care  
  * Affirm sexual identities  
  * Provide social support  
  * Provide a buffering effect against stress  
* Social media can have negative effects on teens when used   
  * Could be associated with changes in parts of the brain related to emotions and learning  
  * Can affect impulse control, social behavior, emotional regulation, and sensitivity to social punishments and rewards  
  * Some correlation between SMU and common mental health concerns   
    * One of the most common interrelationships is between social media and depression  
* Social Media and Depression in Children and Teens (potential explanations)  
  * Social isolation  
    * Today’s children and teens spend less time connecting with friends and family in-person compared w/ past generations   
    * Online social interactions may not be as rewarding  
    * Social media may also make some feel excluded or like they’re missing out when seeing others engage in activities and social interactions  
  * Lack of healthy activities  
    * Physical activity and time spent outdoors help release endorphins, which can help alleviate symptoms of depression  
    * They can also provide a sense of accomplishment and confidence  
    * Teens who spend excessive time on social media may miss out on physical activity   
  * Lack of sleep  
    * Research indicates that sleep deprivation is a common contributor to depression, and social media can have a notable impact on sleep  
    * Scrolling on social media can lead to stress, which can make it hard to sleep  
    * Intention of scrolling for minutes turning into hours   
    * Blue light from electronic screens \-\> suppresses melatonin   
  * Causation unclear, but correlations between SMU and depression have been found and are continuing to be researched  
* Teaching Children and Teens to Have a Healthy Relationship with Social Media  
  * Take regular breaks from digital devices  
  * Turn off notifications  
  * Establish phone-free hours and spaces  
  * Discuss appropriate boundaries and behaviors when interacting online
