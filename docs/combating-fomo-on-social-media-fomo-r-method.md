**Combating Fear of Missing Out (FoMO) on Social Media: The FoMO-R Method** (URL: [https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/17/17/6128](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/17/17/6128))   
*(Published Aug 2020\)* 

* **Authors “develop a FoMO Reduction (FoMO-R) approach that embraces technical elements** such as autoreply, filtering, status**, education on how FoMO occurs, and skills on how to deal with it”** and test it w/ diary study   
    * Specifically, hope to “build the users’ digital resilience and help them to prevent and deal with certain kinds of FoMO”   
        * Interesting study in the sense that it goes beyond identifying commonly reported issue in SMU; instead, accepts its existence and examines a potential solution to mitigate its effects while still engaging with the (also potentially positive) social media environment  
    * **Link to download FoMO-R materials/booklets that participants were provided with: [https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/17/17/6128/s1](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/17/17/6128/s1) (useful when looking for countermeasures)**  
* **Argue that existing approaches to combat FoMO** (often based on counseling) “**overlook the role of social network design features in triggering the problem and**, at the same time, **their potential to play a role in solving it**”   
    
* FoMO: “the preoccupation of users of social media with lost opportunities when they are offline or unable or unwilling to connect and communicate with others to the extent they wish”   
    * **Consequences of FoMO are “lack of sleep, reduced life competency, emotional tension, negative effects on physical well-being, anxiety and a lack of emotional control”**   
    * “**Can lead to compulsive behaviour**, and people may fail in resisting the urge to check their social media or stop being preoccupied about them”   
* Based on the contexts in which FoMO may occur, authors identified five primary fears associated with it:  
1) Fear “of missing the ability to be popular … to others”  
2) Fear “of missing the ability to be … interesting to others”   
3) “Fear of not getting the right interpretation of some social interaction”   
4) “Fear to deal with different social networks at one time”   
5) “Fear of missing temporarily available information and timely interactions”   
* Identified **software-based countermeasures to help reduce these fears in SMU: filtering, autoreply, setting status, and recap**  
    * Also recognized **need for teaching human skills such as expectation management and self-regulation**   
      
* Interesting difference between SMU and pre-social media social interaction: “**before social media, individuals may have attributed the frequency or lack of their interactions with their social group to** the constraints of **not being physically together**,” **meaning individuals would understand one another in not communicating** very **often**. **With the advent of SNS**, however, “**the locus of control for social communication and interaction has altered from partly externally determined to largely internally determined**”   
    * Authors propose that “the **increased social interaction opportunities and** the **easy access to social media may cause** individuals to experience **FoMO**”   
* Note that **social media can play role in procrastination**, to some extent; this exacerbates negative consequences of FoMo-induced social media use   
    
* Researchers’ FoMO-R approach builds on transtheoretical model of behavior change research  
    * Model proposes **five milestones of behavior change (pre-contemplation, contemplation, preparation, action and maintenance)**, and includes core processes of consciousness-raising, counter-conditioning, and stimulus control with regards to how behavior change occurs  
* **Core tools which FoMO-R method provides** to individuals:  
1) **Self talk**  
* Increases individuals’ resistance to temptation  
    * Specifically, “the **use of ‘do not’ can be effective when resisting temptation** and motivating goal-directed behaviour because it provides a sense of psychological empowerment and also conveys force and determination, and is therefore final and not too open to discussion”   
    * Based on this, under the FoMO-R method the **user is instructed to talk to themselves in the structure, “I don’t do X” when they experience FoMO**  
      
2) **Expectation management**  
* Authors claim that individuals may have expectations from online social media use based on previous experiences, such as receiving a certain number of likes or getting responses from those they message immediately   
    * This can then cause users to be more active on social media “to avoid experiencing FoMO that might be the fear of missing the ability to defend their popularity or fear of missing the ability to be interesting”  
* **Use cognitive reappraisal method to help individuals manage expectations** realistically  
    * “individuals can … \[post\] on social media without expecting interaction from others”   
    * “they can clarify to others that they may not reciprocate interaction”   
    * they can “make clear to their social media contacts their current situation, e.g., being busy preparing for an exam or having limited time”   
3) **Enhancing self-esteem**   
* Authors argue “it is necessary to enhance self-esteem to mitigate a certain kind of FoMO"—referring to reliance on Likes and other social media feedback forms for self-reassurance   
    * **Propose the following phrases** for individuals to rely on: **“my popularity is not measured by being active on social media and immediately responding to messages”** and **“I am not the only one who does not receive the interactions they expect”** 

    

**Method involves the following steps:** 

1) **Preparation**: “This stage raises awareness about FoMO and also provides the help-seeker with a greater understanding of the types of FoMO”  
   1) Strategies: Self-rating booklet for identifying types of FoMO experienced  
2) **Planning**: “This stage assists the help-seeker to plan to manage FoMO by providing them with a set of suggested countermeasures. This includes learning … technical countermeasures, … socio countermeasures, … relapse prevention strategies”   
   1) Strategies: List of countermeasures in booklet (e.g.: auto-reply/setting status, managing expectations/self-talk)   
3) **Action**: “Help-seeker applies the selected countermeasure”   
   1) Strategies: Follow countermeasures for fixed time period (week) while applying relapse-prevention strategies including participation in hobbies, positive self-talk, and seeking moral support  
4) **Assessment**: “Help-seeker assesses their satisfaction with the selected countermeasure(s)”   
   1) Strategies: Self-monitoring sheet which allows user to reflect on whether/which countermeasures were effective. If one or more were useful, they proceed to “Review” step. If none were useful, they proceed to “Empowerment” step.   
5) **Empowerment**: “It provides those seeking help with further support to manage their FoMO in the event that the countermeasures selected … fail”   
   1) Strategies: Answer reflective questions on what concrete challenges are making it difficult to manage FoMO (e.g.: “Is peer pressure the cause?” and “Do you put the needs of others above your own?”). Repeat process w/ new countermeasures.  
6) **Review**: “Help-seeker reviews their self-monitoring sheet to assess whether they have managed their FoMO”   
   1) Strategies: Answer reflective questions about whether FoMO management improved. If needed, attempt new countermeasures for different types of FoMO they may experience (w/ same process from start).   
        
* Sample size of diary study was only 30 people, who were then separated into focus groups of 10 where discussion about SMU & FoMO was held before FoMO-R method was introduced by researchers  
    * Participants ages 18-42  
* Diary study about impact of FoMO-R method was conducted over course of ten days, with participants filling out questionnaire after 3 and also maintaining daily diary records  
* Finally, participants completed questionnaire at end of FoMO-R trial period, reflecting on experience with FoMO-R and also giving feedback to researchers on how useful the method coverage & supplemental materials were  
    
* Study measured the effectiveness of FoMO-R by “undertaking a comparative analysis of the FoMO experience before and after using FoMO-R”  
    * Found that “**a significant change was noted in relation to social media before using the FoMO-R … as compared to after using it**”  
        * This was true across all contexts/types of FoMO that the researchers identified  
    * Also found that **significant improvement in awareness levels around managing FoMO occurred in participants using FoMO-R**  
* Conclude: “**these findings suggest that the FoMO-R is expected to work effectively and contribute to helping people to manage their FoMO**. This is achieved **by creating awareness among them of how FoMO occurs on social media and how it can be managed by adopting different countermeasures**, which can be **either technical or social** in nature”   
* Furthermore, researchers used the Treatment Self-Regulation Questionnaire (TSRQ) to assess what motivated participants to continue using the FoMO-R method/adhering to its guidelines  
    * Found that “**participants continued to use this tool without feeling in any way pressurised** or coerced,” potentially indicating that “participants were self-directed and purposefully engaged in FoMO-R, thus exercising a strong sense of choice”   
* Finally, used eTAP questionnaire to measure user intention, attitude, subjective norms, and perceived control regarding use of the FoMO-R method  
    * “Results emerging from all e-TAP factors are positive, which indicates the possibility that the FoMO-R method will be adopted in the future. Essentially, **participants perceive that the FoMO-R is a useful approach in helping them to manage their FoMO more effectively**”
