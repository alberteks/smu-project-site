**The Impact of Social Media on the Mental Health of Adolescents and Young Adults: A Systematic Review** (URL: [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10476631/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10476631/))   
*(Published August 2023\)*

* **The social & personal life of young people is heavily influenced by social media**  
* **Privacy concerns, cyberbullying, and bad effects on schooling and mental health are all risks associated with adolescent social media usage**  
* **Ethical social media use can expand opportunities for connection and conversation \+ boost self esteem, promote health, and gain access to critical medical information**  
* “Youth use cell phones and other forms of media in large numbers, resulting in chronic sleep loss, which has a negative influence on cognitive ability, school performance, and socio-emotional functioning”  
  * “According to data from several cross-sectional, longitudinal, and empirical research, smartphone and social media use among teenagers relates to an increase in mental distress, self-harming behaviors, and suicidality”  
* Being socially linked w/ others helps alleviate stress, worry, and melancholy  
  * **Social media may have positive effects on connectivity, idea sharing, and online learning**  
* Currently, there is debate over the benefits & drawbacks of social media on mental health  
  * Mental health, health behavior, physical health, & mortality risk are all affected by the quantity/quality of social contacts  
* Social theories discovered that social media use influences mental health by affecting how people interpret, maintain, and interact with their social network  
* Social media allows teens to establish friends, discuss ideas, discover new interests, and try out new kinds of self-expression  
  * On the other hand, adolescents frequently engage in trolling (which can focus on body shaming, individual abilities, language, lifestyle, etc.) for amusement without recognizing the potentially harmful consequences  
    * Effects that result from trolling might cause anxiety, depressive symptoms, stress, feelings of isolation, and suicidal thoughts   
* Purpose of study: identify studies on the influence of technology, primarily social media, on the psychosocial functioning, health, and well-being of adolescents & young adults  
* Methodology: MEDLINE biographical database, PubMed, Google Scholar, CINAHL, and Scopus were searched between 1 January 2000 and 30 May 2023  
  * Social media AND mental health AND adolescents AND young adults were included in the search strategy (impact or relation or effect or influence)  
  * Included studies that examined impact of technology, primarily social media, on the psychosocial functioning, health, and well-being of adolescents & young adults  
  * Narrative synthesis of findings used to analyze data   
  * 34 full-text publications evaluated  
* Systematic review identified 11 studies that examined connection between social media use and depression symptoms in children & adolescents  
  * **Research demonstrated modest but statistically significant association between social media use and depression symptoms** (however, relationship’s causality is unclear – more studies needed)   
* Additional research studied effects of technology on psychosocial functioning, health, and well-being of adolescents and young adults  
  * **Higher levels of social media usage were connected with worse mental health outcomes and associated with an increased risk of internalizing and externalizing difficulties among adolescents, especially females**  
  * **Use of social media was also connected with body image problems and disordered eating, especially among young women, and social media may be a risk factor for alcohol consumption and associated consequences among adolescents and young adults**

*\[Note: see paper for sources on each of the following claims below\]*

* **Cyberbullying victimization is connected with poorer mental health outcomes in teenagers, including an increased risk of sadness and anxiety**  
* **The use of social media was also connected with more depressive symptoms and excessive reassurance-seeking, but also with greater popularity and perceived social support, as well as appearance comparisons and body image worries, especially among young women**  
* **Children and adolescents’ bedtime media device use was substantially related to inadequate sleep quantity, poor sleep quality, and excessive daytime drowsiness**  
* While online friends can be a significant source of social support, **in-person social support appears to provide greater protection against persecution**  
* **Digital and social media use offers both benefits and risks to the health of children and adolescents, and an individualized family media use plan can help strike a balance between screen time/online time and other activities, set boundaries for accessing content, promote digital literacy, and support open family communication and consistent media use rules**  
* Discussion: Does Social Media Have a Positive or Negative Impact on Adolescents and Young Adults?  
  * **Effects of social media on mental health are complex, as different goals are served by different behaviors & different outcomes are produced by distinct patterns of use**  
    * Empirical uncertainty is exacerbated by the fact that there are not many documented metrics of technology is used  
    * Self-reports are most commonly used for measuring tech use, but they can be prone to inaccuracy (they are based on people’s own perceptions of their behavior, and these perceptions can be inaccurate)  
      * At best, there is simply a weak correlation between self-reported smartphone usage patterns and levels that have been objectively verified   
  * **To utilize and respond to social media in either an adaptive or maladaptive manner, it is crucial to first have a solid understanding of personal qualities that some children may be more likely to exhibit than others**  
  * **Specific behaviors or experiences on social media that put teenagers in danger need to be recognized**   
* When a previous study particularly questioned teenagers in the U.S., the authors found that 31% of them believe the consequences are predominantly good, 45% believe they are neither positive nor harmful, and 24% believe they are unfavorable  
  * Teens who considered social media beneficial reported that they were able to interact w/ friends, learn new things, and meet individuals who shared similar interests because of it  
  * Social media is said to enhance the possibility of bullying, ignoring face-to-face contact, and obtaining incorrect beliefs about the lives of other people, according to those who believe the ramifications are serious  
* The Dangers That Come With Young Adults Utilizing Social Media:  
  * **Peer relationships in the world of social media can be more frequent, intense, and rapid than in real life**  
  * **Being the target of cyberbullying has been shown to relate to greater rates of self-inflicted damage, suicidal ideation, and a variety of other internalizing & externalizing issues**  
  * **Young people may be put in danger by the peer pressure that can be found on social networking platforms**  
    * This can take the form of being rejected by peers, engaging in online fights, or being involved in drama or conflict  
    * Teens who spend more time online have access to a wider diversity of their peers as well as content that could be damaging to them  
    * If young people are exposed to information on social media that depicts risky behavior (e.g., drinking, using drugs), their likelihood of engaging in such behaviors themselves may increase  
    * It may be simple to access online materials that deal with self-harm and suicide, which may result in an increase in the risk of self-harm among adolescents who are already at risk  
      * Study found that 14.8% of young people who were admitted to mental hospitals because they posed a risk to others/themselves had viewed internet sites that encouraged suicide in the two weeks leading up to their admission  
    * Constant favorable feeds of friends can influence certain young individuals, leading them to make unfavorable comparisons between themselves and others (whether about abilities, achievements, appearance, etc.)  
  * **There is a correlation between higher levels of social networking in comparison and depressed symptoms in adolescents, according to studies**  
  * **Past studies have shown consistent connections between using a mobile device before bed & poorer sleep quality results**  
    * Notably, 36% of adolescents claim they wake up at least once over the course of the night to check their electronic devices, and 40% of adolescents say they use a mobile device within five minutes of going to bed  
  * **Most of the research in study found a positive and reciprocal link between the use of social media and feelings of depression and, on occasion, suicidal ideation**  
    * Additionally, it is unknown to what extent the vulnerability of teens and the characteristics of substance use affect this connection (or other environmental factors)  
  * Even if it is probable that moderate use relates to improved self-regulation, it is not apparent whether this is the result of intermediate users having naturally greater self-regulation  
* Gains From Social Media:  
  * Using social media may present opportunities for humor & entertainment, identity formation, & creative expression  
  * Social connections is a big benefit – **81% of students say social media boosts their sense of connectedness to others**  
    * **Social media can be used to increase acceptance or a feeling of community by providing adolescents w/ opportunities to connect w/ others who share their interests, beliefs, and experiences**  
  * **Digital media has the potential to improve adolescent mental health in a variety of ways, including cutting-edge applications in medical screening, treatment, & prevention**  
    * Past research has suggested that perusing social media pages for signs of melancholy or drug abuse may be viable
