**Teens, screens, and mental health (WHO)**  
(URL: [https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/25-09-2024-teens--screens-and-mental-health](https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/25-09-2024-teens--screens-and-mental-health))  
*Published September 2024*

* Survey conducted assessing the well-being of school-aged (11, 12, and 15 y/o) children across 44 countries and regions in Europe, central Asia, and Canada  
    * “More than 1 in 10 adolescents (11%) showed signs of problematic social media behaviour, struggling to control their use and experiencing negative consequences. Girls reported higher levels of problematic social media use than boys (13% vs 9%)”  
        * (Problematic social media use \= pattern of behavior characterized by addiction-like symptoms, including an inability to control social media usage, experiencing withdrawal when not using it, neglecting other activities in favor of social media, and facing negative consequences in daily life due to excessive use)  
    * “Over a third (36%) of young people reported constant contact with friends online, with the highest rates among 15-year-old girls (44%)”  
* Previous research found that problematic social media users also reported lower mental and social well-being and higher levels of substance use compared to non-problematic users and non-users  
    * Long-term negative consequences  
    * Problematic SMU also associated w/ less sleep & later bedtimes, potentially impacting adolescents’ overall health & academic performance  
* “... It’s clear we need immediate and sustained action to help adolescents turn the tide on potentially damaging social media use, which has been shown to lead to depression, bullying, anxiety and poor academic performance.” –Dr. Hans Henri P. Kluge, WHO Regional Director for Europe  
    * Digital literacy education is so important, yet it remains inadequate in many countries, and where it is available, it often fails to keep pace w/ young people and rapidly evolving technology  
* Positive aspects of social media use  
    * Adolescents who are heavy but non-problematic users reported stronger peer support and social connections  
    * “...\[adolescents\] should rule social media, and not have social media ruling them.”  
* Roadmap for action  
    * Invest in a health promoting school environment, in which digital literacy education is embedded  
        * Implement evidence-based programs in schools covering responsible social media use, online safety, critical thinking, and healthy habits  
    * Enhance mental health services  
        * Ensure access to confidential, non-judgmental and affordable mental health services which can address issues arising from problematic digital technology use  
    * Promote open dialogue  
        * Encourage conversations about digital well-being within families, schools and communities to reduce stigma and increase awareness  
    * Train educators and healthcare providers  
        * Provide specialized training to deliver effective and inclusive digital literacy education and support  
    * Enhance platform accountability (more regulatory/legislation-oriented)  
        * Ensure social media platforms enforce age restrictions and create a regulatory framework encouraging responsible design of digital tools for young users
