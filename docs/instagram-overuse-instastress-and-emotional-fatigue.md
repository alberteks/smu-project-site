**From Instagram overuse to instastress and emotional fatigue: the mediation of addiction** (URL: [https://doi.org/10.1108/SJME-12-2018-0059](https://doi.org/10.1108/SJME-12-2018-0059)) 

*(Published Sep 2019\)*

* Study investigates “specifically the consequences of excessive Instagram use”   
  * Authors acknowledge benefits of social media based on past literature, including increased consumer-customer interaction, civic engagement, and learning processes  
    * At the same time, they cite literature indicating that technological use can have addictive/overuse properties, and refer to the “paradoxes of technological products framework proposed by Mick and Fournier (1998).” This framework posits that many technologies have a simultaneous positive and negative side, and the researchers cite previous literature which used the framework to explain “some of the counterproductive effects of individuals’ engagement with SNSs”   
* Technostress: “the negative effects that the use of computer-based information and communication technologies directly or indirectly generate on individuals’ attitudes, thoughts or behaviors (Weil and Rosen, 1997)”   
  * Cite “overidentification with computer technologies … looking always for efficiency and speed” as relevant mechanism of technostress for Instagram/modern SNS use  
* “Instastress addresses the need to stay updated so as not to miss any relevant information  
* “Emotional fatigue refers to managing the information overload”   
  * Authors hypothesize that “heavy SNSs users may \[experience\] emotional (mental) fatigue due to being overcharged with new information”  
* Authors differentiate between Instagram overuse and SNS addiction by defining addiction as compulsive SNS use (“related to individuals’ inability to control his/her own internet behavior”)   
    
* Collected data via self-administered questionnaire, w/ initial sample of 360 and data analyzed from 342 respondents (average age 22 y.o., \>85% students, respondents were “young Spaniards aged over 18”)   
* Respondents reported checking Instagram an average of 32 times/day, and spending 2.5 hrs connected to Instagram on average   
* Questionnaire assessed variables of interest (overuse, instastress, addiction, and emotional fatigue) on a Likert scale of 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 5 (“strongly agree”)  
  * Used scales for each of these variables that had been verified by previous literature/studies  
      
* “The results confirmed the hypothesized direct positive relationship … between overuse and emotional fatigue”  
* Relationships found with p \< 0.001 (very statistically significant):  
  * Overuse → addiction  
  * Overuse → emotional fatigue   
  * Addiction → emotional fatigue  
  * Addiction → instastress  
* Note that, unlike the previously listed relationships, the hypothesized overuse → instastress was found statistically insignificant   
* Results also showed that “addiction to Instagram was mainly due to the fact that the respondents spent more time on Instagram than initially intended, which results in their incapability of reducing its usage”  
  * At the same time, “the risk of losing a significant relationship, job or career opportunity was not found to be a significant consequence of their addictive Instagram usage”   
    * Suggests that, while Instagram addiction may have negative correlates beyond just taking up time of users–such as emotional fatigue and instastress–it is not as detrimental as other addictions in its interference in users’ daily lives   
* Overall (to the end of their research question), authors concluded that “addiction partially mediates the impact of overuse on emotional fatigue and instastress”  
  * Essentially, this means that overuse can often lead to addiction, which then leads to negative consequences of Instagram use. However, the only partial mediation shows that simply overuse, without a user necessarily reaching the threshold of addiction, correlates directly to negative consequences as well  
      
* Researchers suggest two groups of preventative measures for consideration: treatment and prevention strategies   
  * For treatment, they suggest that “SNS addiction could be viewed as a new type of pathology that requires comprehensive treatment by professionals, and addicted users should look for help from these professionals” based on findings that addiction leads to some factors overuse does not (like instastress)  
  * For prevention, they cite the negative consequences of overuse and not just addiction (emotional fatigue) and suggest that social network site managers should display information “about the possible addictive facet of Instagram usage” when people sign up for an account or when new features are promoted  
    * For example of wording, authors write: ‘Users should be required to read about them and accept the “potential side effects of an excessive use”’  
    * They also suggest that role models are crucial for young and impressionable Instagram users: “Parents (and teachers) should not only teach the youngest how to use SNS, but should also lead by example regarding the use of new technologies and SNS”  
      * Encouraging offline hobbies and setting limits on SNS use in family are both parental approaches suggested by authors
