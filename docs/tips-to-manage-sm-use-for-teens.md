**Tips to Manage Social Media Use for Teens (American Academy of Pediatrics)** (URL: [https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/center-of-excellence-on-social-media-and-youth-mental-health/qa-portal/qa-portal-library/qa-portal-library-questions/tips-to-manage-social-media-use-for-teens/](https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/center-of-excellence-on-social-media-and-youth-mental-health/qa-portal/qa-portal-library/qa-portal-library-questions/tips-to-manage-social-media-use-for-teens/)) 

* “Currently, there is no evidence to support the claim that social media is universally addictive, destructive, or harmful to the mental health of all adolescents” ([https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-018-02109-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-018-02109-8))   
    * **Systematic review on social media use and well-being only shows weak association with poor health/well-being**  
    * **Impact varies from person-to-person**  
* **Amount of time adolescents spend on social media does not determine whether they are “addicted” to it**   
    * **Duration only explains 6% of problematic internet use** ([https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563216302059](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563216302059))   
* **Feeling shame or primarily negative view of one’s own social media use may lead to adverse effects on well-being**  
    * “For participants who believed Facebook was good for them and society, greater time spent on social media was not associated with well-being. However, for participants who believed that Facebook was bad for them and for society, greater time spent on social media was linked to lower well-being” ([https://research.facebook.com/publications/mindsets-matter-how-beliefs-about-facebook-moderate-the-association-between-time-spent-and-well-being/](https://research.facebook.com/publications/mindsets-matter-how-beliefs-about-facebook-moderate-the-association-between-time-spent-and-well-being/))   
* “... High schoolers and college students showed **higher activity in brain regions associated with reward when they received likes from unknown peers** **on their Instagram \[posts\]**…” ([https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27247125/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27247125/)) “...as well as when they viewed others’ images with likes and provided likes to other peers’ photos on Instagram” [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29982823/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29982823/)   
* “Adolescents who check social media 15 or more times per day show different neural responses to anticipating reward or feedback compared to adolescents who do not check social media at all” [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36595277/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36595277/)   
    * Regard above studies with caution, methodology might be flawed  
* **Social media may be used in positive ways, fostering social connection:**  
    * Texting friends  
    * Facilitating self-disclosure, validation, companionship, instrumental support, and conflict resolution from online communication ([https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40894-017-0059-y](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40894-017-0059-y))   
    * Opportunities to share interests and activities, which can help teens better understand who they are ([https://socialmedialab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22976/files/media/file/luo-copsych-self-disclosure.pdf](https://socialmedialab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22976/files/media/file/luo-copsych-self-disclosure.pdf))   
    * Helpful in shaping teens’ racial or ethnic identity through observing media representation of individuals from different racial/ethnic identities ([https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/9781119011071.iemp0083](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/9781119011071.iemp0083))   
    * Important for sexuality and gender diverse (SGD) youth where they can have a safe place to be their authentic selves without stigma ([https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0743558419884700](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0743558419884700))   
    * Good for staying up-to-date on current events and participating in civic and political activities ([https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/center-of-excellence-on-social-media-and-youth-mental-health/qa-portal/qa-portal-library/qa-portal-library-questions/current-events-and-social-media/](https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/center-of-excellence-on-social-media-and-youth-mental-health/qa-portal/qa-portal-library/qa-portal-library-questions/current-events-and-social-media/))   
* Mixed findings about effectiveness of limiting social media use; **setting limits is beneficial for some, could be “detrimental” to others depending how to use it** ([https://guilfordjournals.com/doi/10.1521/jscp.2021.40.1.46](https://guilfordjournals.com/doi/10.1521/jscp.2021.40.1.46))   
* Tips on reducing social media use:  
    * Set focus times, switch on “do-not-disturb” or “focus mode” during times such as designated homework time, school, or bedtime  
    * Develop schedule including time for offline hobbies/interests  
    * Plan regular sleep routine that helps with relaxation  
    * Consider talking to family about establishing Family Media Plan that can provide ideas for rules, guidelines, and shared media experiences  
    * Explore variety of control settings and understand how to curate feed and see beneficial content ([https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/center-of-excellence-on-social-media-and-youth-mental-health/qa-portal/qa-portal-library/qa-portal-library-questions/managing-content-on-social-media-feed/](https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/center-of-excellence-on-social-media-and-youth-mental-health/qa-portal/qa-portal-library/qa-portal-library-questions/managing-content-on-social-media-feed/))
