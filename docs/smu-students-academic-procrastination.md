**Social Media User Students’ Academic Procrastination** (URL: [https://www.academia.edu/download/93880533/pdf\_40.pdf](https://www.academia.edu/download/93880533/pdf_40.pdf))   
*(Published Dec 2018\)*

- Correlational study investigating potential relationship between academic procrastination and social media usage intensity  
- Population: 419 students at Semarang State University in Indonesia  
- Social media use was measured through the Social Media Usage Intensity Scale, while procrastination was measured through the Academic Procrastination Scale  
    
- Out of the student cohort of the study, “**62 respondents were categorized as having the low intensity of social media usage, 297 respondents were categorized as having  the moderate intensity of social media usage, and 60 respondents were categorized as having a high intensity of social media usage**” (54-55)  
- The **majority of respondents were** also **found to have moderate procrastination levels** (288)  
- Pearson product-moment correlation found that **“there is a relationship between procrastination and social media usage among the students of Semarang State University,” which was statistically significant** (55)  
  - **“The higher the students’ academic procrastination, the higher the intensity of social media usage, and vice versa”** (55)  
- Note that purely correlational nature of study means the **relationship does not necessarily mean causation**; the authors cite many factors from scientific literature that can mediate relationships w/ procrastination, as well as academic performance
