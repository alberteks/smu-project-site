Original study:   
**How do social media use, gaming frequency, and internalizing symptoms predict each other over time in early-to-middle adolescence?**  
[https://academic.oup.com/jpubhealth/advance-article/doi/10.1093/pubmed/fdaf150/8371934?login=false](https://academic.oup.com/jpubhealth/advance-article/doi/10.1093/pubmed/fdaf150/8371934?login=false)   
*(Published December 2025\)*

Study findings:

* **Methods:** separating within-person from between-person effects, random-intercept cross-lagged panel model was applied to three annual waves of data (T1, T2, T3) on social media use, gaming, & internalizing symptoms among 629 adolescents (51% girls, average age 12 years, 7 months) in Greater Manchester, England  
* **Results:** longitudinal relationships varied by gender → more frequent gaming at T2 predicted less time spent on social media use at T3 in girls (but not boys), & more frequent internalizing symptoms at T2 predicted reductions in gaming frequency at T3 in boys (but not girls). No evidence that time spent on social media or gaming frequency predicted later internalizing symptoms among girls or boys. Sensitivity analyses that distinguished active vs. passive social media use replicated findings  
* **Conclusions:** findings do not support widely held view that adolescent technology use is a major casual factor in their mental health difficulties  
* **Limitations: “...**Study relied on self-reported data and a 12-month lag between measurements, which may limit detection of important short-term, reciprocal associations between digital technology use and internalizing symptoms. Adolescent engagement with social media and gaming, as well as their emotional states, is known to exhibit considerable variability on a daily, or even hourly, basis.” Study also did not differentiate between various types of games or social media 

Commentary from experts on PBS:

* A lot of possible colliding factors for generational behavioral changes BESIDES SMU/screen time: could be social influences from other children, more parents working outside of home, TV shows, music, drinking, school pressure, lack of being able to be w/ a lot of generations of family, lack of affordability of childcare, etc.   
* Stress from constant social media notifications could be associated but not predictable  
  * Getting distracted in class from time-to-time by notifs does not necessarily lead to pitfalls, behavioral issues, or intellectual defects   
* More than just time spent on social media, content which children consume seems to matter as well, as well as timing of use (e.g., is it interfering with sleep? Are they exposed to content in the news or online victimization that may be contributing to some stressors?)  
* Advice to parents for managing adolescents’ social media use  
  * Create consistent, intentional boundaries for use  
  * Parent pacts to collectively give middle schoolers flip/landline phones so kids feel connected even without social media  
  * Have ongoing conversations about phones/social media  
    * E.g.,   
      * What are some exciting things you have going on?  
      * What kinds of social connections are you bridging?  
      * What kinds of new skills are you learning?  
      * What kinds of new explorations are benefitting your academics?  
      * What are some things that are really distressing you? (e.g., too much news, doomscrolling, advertisements affecting body image)  
  * Using built-in features on social media to block harmful content
