**Social Media Use and Friendship Closeness in Adolescents’ Daily Lives: An Experience Sampling Study** (URL: [https://psycnet.apa.org/fulltext/2021-14131-010.html](https://psycnet.apa.org/fulltext/2021-14131-010.html))

*(Published Feb 2021\)*

* Group of 387 adolescents (mean age 14.11 y.o.) reported “Instagram, WhatsApp, and Snapchat use in the previous hour and their momentary experiences of friendship closeness,” 6 times a day for 3 weeks  
  * Subjects were primarily in “middle adolescence (13 years to 15 years),” with the authors examining “the period in which friends become more supportive and important” and how social media might play a role in this transition  
  * Responded to questions through experience sampling method (ESM), in an app called Ethica installed on their phones  
* Note that study took place in the Netherlands/with primarily Dutch adolescents. Results might therefore vary in comparison to other countries (such as which social media apps are more or less popular among the adolescent group)   
* Authors cite how “close friendships are characterized by supportiveness, accessibility, and responsiveness, and fulfill adolescents’ need for intimacy,” and aimed to examine whether social media provides similar closeness (and therefore psychological benefits) with adolescents’ friends online  
* In addition to analyzing how individuals’ friendship closeness was impacted by social media use throughout the day, authors compared how specifically interactions with close friends vs. general social media use impacted any association w/ friendship closeness  
  * Idea is that only some types/uses of social media (if there is a correlation) might be beneficial for friendship closeness. Study hoped to shed light on this, building on hypothesis from [previous research](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.4324/9780429468360-2/buddies-friends-followers-joanna-yau-stephanie-reich) by different authors  
* Finally, study aimed to compare Instagram, WhatsApp, and Snapchat effects to see if different social media apps might yield different associations (and by extension, presumably be more/less beneficial to close adolescent friendships)  
    
* Measured friendship closeness with 7-point scale response to question “How close to your close friends do you feel right now?”  
  * “Adolescents in this sample defined close friends as peers with whom they spend time and have fun, who provide support and whom they can trust and count on, with whom they feel at ease to disclose intimate personal information, and with whom they can be their authentic self”  
* Measured social media use via Ethica app, asking adolescents to indicate how much time they had spent on the three social media apps in the study (Instagram, WhatsApp, and Snapchat) in the previous hour. Survey also distinguished between multiple actions done in each app (for example in Instagram, items were “viewing posts/stories of others,” “reading direct messages,” and sending direct messages).  
* Finally, survey included question about whether adolescents had stayed in touch with close friends within the last hour, and distinguished again between each of the three apps and also face-to-face interaction  
  * Used specific phrasing “in touch” based on interviews with 34 participants determining context of SMU and related jargon prior to the study. Useful to know study took such precautions since self-reporting can potentially produce different results based on different phrasing  
      
* Survey found that, “**in total, 345 adolescents (89%) used Instagram at least once a week, 375 (97%) used WhatsApp, and 285 (73%) used Snapchat at least once a week**”  
  * Reaffirms other studies’ claims that SMU is almost ubiquitous amongst adolescents now  
* **Around 50% of adolescents’ use of Instagram and Snapchat involved being in touch with their friends**   
  * Interestingly (to me, given that Whatsapp is primarily a messaging application), **participants had been in touch with their friends** “**only during 23% of all occasions when** adolescents had **used WhatsApp**”  
    * Potentially indicates social media is a preferred method for close-friend communication in modern adolescents compared to traditional messaging apps  
  * Authors summarized: “although WhatsApp was used by the largest group of participants, they used WhatsApp relatively less frequently with close friends than Instagram and Snapchat”   
      
* The average amount of time adolescents spent on Instagram across the 3-week survey period did not significantly relate to their mean level of friendship closeness over that period  
  * Interaction w/o close friends in particular on Instagram did not relate significantly to friendship closeness over a long period, while “adolescents who had been in touch more frequently with their close friends via Instagram across the 3 weeks felt, on average, closer to their friends than adolescents who had been in touch less frequently with close friends via Instagram”  
  * However, there was a very small but significant negative effect on adolescents’ feelings of friendship closeness immediately after using Instagram (“adolescents felt less close to their friends after they had used Instagram in the previous hour”)  
    * This was true both for Instagram interaction with self-identified “close friends,” and also for other uses of the app  
* Overall, **in the short term Instagram caused a perceived decrease in friendship closeness**. However, **Instagram use without close friends did not impact friendship closeness over a longer period**, and **Instagram use with close friends** it actually **improved how close they felt to one another**  
    
* Independently of whether adolescents used WhatsApp with their close friends or without, **using WhatsApp more frequently over the course of three weeks caused adolescents to feel closer to one another on average** compared to those who used it less frequently. However, in a similar pattern to Instagram use **in the short-term adolescents using WhatsApp** in the previous hour self-**reported feeling less close to friends compared to not using it at all**   
    
* **For Snapchat**, the authors actually found **no significant impact of the app’s use on friendship closeness**, both in the long-term and for momentary effects right after using it  
    
* The authors summarize findings across platforms: “Looking at differences between platforms, at the between-person level … we found moderately strong positive significant associations of friendship closeness with Instagram use with close friends (β \= .171) and general WhatsApp use (β \= .104), but not for Snapchat use. In contrast, at the within-person level … friendship closeness was significantly negatively related to Instagram use (β \= −.058) and WhatsApp use (β \= −.027)”  
* After further statistical analysis, the authors also concluded that “**there was significant heterogeneity between adolescents in the strength and direction of the association of friendship closeness with Instagram, WhatsApp, and Snapchat use**”  
  * Indicates that person-to-person impacts of social media are very different and whether its use is positive or negative significantly depends on the individual  
  * Also (personal note, not mentioned in paper) provides tangential supporting evidence for models such as [windows of development](windows-of-developmental-sensitivity-to-SM.md), given the age variance of a few years for participants in this study  
* Unlike many other studies on SMU, **study found the relationship between most of the social media platforms’ use and friendship closeness was not different based on participant gender**. The only exception to this was that “within-person association of WhatsApp use with friendship closeness was significantly negative among boys … and nonsignificant among girls”
