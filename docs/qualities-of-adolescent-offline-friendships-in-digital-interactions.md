**Are the Qualities of Adolescents’ Offline Friendships Present in Digital Interactions?** (URL: [https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40894-017-0059-y](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40894-017-0059-y))   
*(Published May 2017\)*

Summary:  
There are many similarities between adolescents’ offline and online friendships. While the behaviors and activities (e.g., liking, posting, sending selfies) are novel, they largely serve the same purpose as those offline. Digital communication may increase ramifications of conflict due to permanence & the speed by which information travels, but at the core, friendships seem to have the same key characteristics (self disclosure, validation, companionship, instrumental support, conflict, & conflict resolution) because teens often interact w/ individuals they also consider friends offline. Furthermore, digital communication can provide more opportunities for friends to disclose, spend time together, and display affection than in offline spaces alone.

* High quality, intimate, friendships are associated w/ social & emotional well-being  
    * Having friends can buffer against the negative effects of families that are not emotionally supportive, peer victimization, & school transitions  
    * Adolescent friendships are centered on loyalty, intimacy, & commitment  
        * They play an important role in adolescents’ identity development because self-disclosure facilitates self-exploration & understanding  
    * Six identified distinguishing categories of friendship:  
        * Self-disclosure (i.e., communicating information to one another)  
        * Validation (i.e., increasing the self-worth of another by demonstrating care and wanding the other to feel special)  
        * Companionship (i.e., voluntarily spending time together)  
        * Instrumental support (i.e., providing resources or services towards one another)  
        * Conflict   
        * Conflict resolution   
* Digital spaces characterized by unique properties such as the potential visibility and permanence of shared information & the ease by which shared information can be duplicated (which may alter the characteristics of friendship)  
    * Most social media apps offer a variety of channels for users to interact w/ friends; users can broadcast information to their entire network, leave comments on friends’ profiles that are viewable by others in their network, & send private messages  
    * It is challenging to quantify friendships online  
* In this review, term “digital” includes both online text/SMS interactions  
* Digital interactions differ from face-to-face interactions in numerous ways  
    * Users have fewer audiovisual cues such as facial expressions & tone of voice to rely on when determining the meaning of a digital message  
    * Online interactions between friends are more permanent–users “like” content shared by friends–and broadly viewed–the number of likes a post receives are viewable by others  
* Methods:  
    * Identified digital parallels of behaviors/interactions by cross-listing six core qualities of adolescent friendships w/ the keywords “friends” or “friendships” and w/ different forms of technology-mediated communication or specific social media apps  
    * Studies specifically examining romantic relationships & studies w/o a least a portion of the participants between the ages 12 and 18 not included in the study  
* Results:  
    * Self-disclosure  
        * Similar to face-to-face disclosures, digital disclosures can help adolescents seek support & cope w/ emotional distress  
        * Studies found, from both self-report and content analyses of instant messages, that disclosure via technology-mediated communication could reduce negative emotions  
        * On Snapchat mundane disclosures (common in in-person self disclosure) are also shared often 	  
            * Common images included funny faces, selfies indicating the sender’s current mood, and everyday moments such as cute pets, food, & haircuts  
            * This suggests that the disclosure of mundane information online occurs frequently, like face-to-face settings, and may facilitate greater intimacy because the senders are presenting themselves in a vulnerable position  
        * Just as adolescents would share intimate info directly w/ friends rather than broadcasting it at a party or in the classroom, teens seemed to opt to disclose intimate info through direct forms of communication such as DMs  
            * On average, teens reported that interaction partners on Snapchat are perceived as being closer than partners whom they interacted w/ through other more open forms of social media such as Instagram, Twitter, & Facebook  
        * One study found that, online, teens’ interactions were primarily w/ friends, the majority of which were with “very good friends”   
            * Logging study of college students’ Facebook activities found that these students messaged a very small percentage of their network in a given week, potentially friends rather than acquaintances  
        * Self-disclosure promotes self-clarification, and this has also been identified online, but largely on spaces where adolescents can remain anonymous  
            * Teen bloggers wrote about sexuality, as well as other intimate topics  
            * On platforms where identities are known, studies find that self-exploration is much more constrained by social norms and gender stereotypes than on chat rooms and blogs (college students tended to present themselves in ways that they thought would appeal to their networks of friends & acquaintances)  
    * Validation  
        * On social media platforms, adolescents validate their friends through affectionate language and lavish compliments   
            * Also includes liking friends’ posts   
                * Some even characterize liking posts as a form of virtual gift-giving; individuals give out likes on Facebook as they would give out gifts offline  
        * Adolescents also refer to one another by nicknames & write elaborate messages containing shared memories to their friends on Facebook & Instagram  
        * Creating digital birthday collages are a novel way of communicating made possible by technology, replicating how friends may already interact offline  
        * Validation between friends seems to be more common among girls, and this is also true in digital interactions  
            * While there were no gender differences in the total \# of texts sent, girls sent more texts w/ positive or neutral content than boys did  
            * In a study of adult Facebook users, women reported a higher frequency of liking others’ posts than men  
                * Similarly, in focus groups w/ teens, girls were more likely to report expecting their close friends to like their posts on Facebook/Instagram & asking for likes if their friends did not yet do so  
                * Boys were more likely to like posts based on the content rather than on the relationship between them and the poster  
            * In offline studies, validation has included complimenting, caring for one another, & celebrating accomplishments  
        * Teens can seek reassurance from their friends on social media through likes & comments w/o having to ask their friends’ opinions on them overtly and potentially risk jeopardizing their friendship  
    * Companionship  
        * Research on offline friendships indicates that interactions between friends are fun and relaxing, and this has also been observed in studies of companionship online  
        * Young adults & teens share jokes, funny videos, images w/ friends, status updates, etc. with friends online  
            * Both young adults & teens also reported “stalking” or browsing other people’s Facebook profiles w/ their friends as a form of entertainment  
        * Interactions between individuals may be more limited in some aspects than they would be in offline settings (e.g., users cannot see each others’ expressions)  
            * College students reported that they could still experience closeness & sincerity through instant messaging because of its synchronous nature; users could receive a response instantaneously even though they were not in the same physical space  
        * While the effects of tech-mediated communication may not be equivalent to those of face-to-face interactions, adolescents still may find that tech-mediated communication benefits their friendships  
    * Instrumental Support  
        * Teens use instant messaging & Facebook to ask for help w/ homework and discuss assignments and Snapchat to ask friends for feedback while shopping  
        * Instrumental support also includes protecting friends who are being bothered by others  
            * In digital spaces, adolescents reported being more likely to defend & support victims of cyberbullying if they were friends w/ the victim or had a positive relationship w/ them  
            * However, teens reported that perceived expectations for support from friends usually consisted of comforting the victim and not reinforcing the bully, rather than actually confronting the bully  
                * Oftentimes, support was provided offline, rather than online as well  
        * Teens use online spaces to ask for support for offline tasks (e.g., homework) when they cannot ask in-person and they also use offline spaces to resolve issues that arise online (e.g., cyberbullying)  
    * Conflict  
        * Common causes of conflicts identified on literature on offline friendships are public disrespect such as betrayal and being ignored publicly, and undependability, such as lack of closeness and failure to provide instrumental support   
            * Conflicts online seem to also be frequently attributed to these causes  
        * Instances of betrayal online included spreading rumors & the unwanted distribution of information that had been sent privately  
        * Features, like anonymity, resulted in increased conflict following mean comments  
        * Technology-mediated communication has enabled messages & images to be easily saved and rapidly distributed to a wider audience, intensifying the harm betrayal can cause  
        * Instances of being ignored publicly by friends were also identified online  
            * “Top friends,” “close friends,” etc. feature caused conflict between teens as this was a public declaration of who mattered most to them; not making the list or not being ranked first suggested that friendship was unimportant, or at least less important than other friendships  
            * Much like not being invited to parties or being neglected in favor of other friends/romantic partners offline, online features aimed at emphasizing exclusivity in relationships can lead to jealousy and exclusion  
        * Akin to offline friendships, conflicts can arise because of undependability; for example, adolescents have reported that conflicts sometimes occur if they did not respond to texts immediately  
            * This problem was exacerbated by messaging apps such as Facebook Messenger or WhatsApp that indicate to the sender that the recipient has opened the message  
        * In offline studies, causes of conflicts that are most prevalent vary by age: younger adolescents reported more conflicts over public disrespect & undependability whereas older adolescents reported more conflicts over private disrespect  
            * Public disrespect is also frequently mentioned nonetheless, even among college students, who reported conflict occurring because a friend shared a photo of them looking unattractive or intoxicated  
    * Conflict Resolution  
        * While some college students reported that it is easier to discuss relationship challenges on social media than by phone or face-to-face, more research is needed on how incidences of conflict between friends on digital media are resolved   
            * Some adolescents may prefer to resolve conflicts face-to-face, because of the presence of non-verbal cues to indicate the other’s mood  
* **Clear evidence that the qualities of friendships persist in digital interactions**  
    * Across many types of platforms (e.g., primarily image based vs. primarily text based and direct messaging vs. broadcast) adolescents:   
        * **Disclose more intimate information with friends than with acquaintances**  
        * **Validate one another through the use of supportive language and recognition in features such as likes and comments**  
        * **Spend time with one another, and provide instrumental support**  
* **Conflicts that occur through technology-mediated communication (e.g., sharing information that was disclosed privately, designating another friend as the Top Friend, not responding immediately) are also similar to the causes of friendship conflict offline (e.g., betrayal, exclusion)**  
* **It seems that adolescents use these platforms as an extension of their offline interactions, as many of the digital interactions have parallel behaviors offline**   
* **While the characteristics of friendship remain the same across modalities, digital interactions offer increased benefits in some areas while posing additional risks in others**  
    * **Technology-mediated communication facilitates self-disclosure**  
        * Adolescents reported preferring to share intimate information via text messaging than in-person because text allows more time to craft responses and more ways to control emotions/calm down  
    * Adolescents w/ social anxiety especially perceived online communication as being valuable for self-disclosure, and among boys with social anxiety, those who used IM or chat rooms reported higher friendship quality than those who used these platforms infrequently   
    * Digital media may also facilitate greater companionship between offline friends because they enable adolescents to circumvent the common restrictions to communication, thereby facilitating constant communication   
    * Digital media enables adolescents to continue discussing the day’s events & to interact w/ friends w/o adult monitoring  
    * Damage to a friendship caused by one individual spreading gossip/rumors about the other may be far greater online because they can spread faster and be seen by more people on digital spaces   
    * Overt display & permanence of likes & comments may be more validating than comments made face-to-face as individuals can refer back and remember what was said, but they can also be more damaging as individuals may feel that they did not receive enough, perhaps in comparison to others in their network
