**Online Social Networking in Adolescence: Patterns of Use in Six European Countries and Links With Psychosocial Functioning** (URL \- [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jadohealth.2013.11.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jadohealth.2013.11.010))

\*Note: study from 2014 so very not-up-to-date. Will search for more research on this topic, just a starting point w/ large sample

*Summary: Heavier social media use amongst adolescents has a pronounced negative effect on academic performance and offline activities/hobbies, irrespective of age. The study discusses how this is likely due to displacement of offline time (see [Albert-Selin Project Guide](can-too-much-screen-time-harm-you.md)for statistics on time spent online) and lack of peer-to-peer interaction. Younger users also have a significant correlation between social media usage and internalizing behavior problems, more so than older users–suggesting greater maturity helps avoid negative effects. To this end, older adolescents (16 and over) may actually get social competency benefits from social media use.*

**Context**

* Sample from 6 countries, with 10,930 adolescents from ages 14-17. “Younger adolescents” were defined as individuals below 15.9 years of age, and “older adolescents” as individuals 16 and above  
* Based on self-reporting use at the time, use of social networking sites (SNS, acronym used from here) was categorized into moderate use (\<2 hours) and heavy use (\>=2 hours)  
* Study then used Youth Self Report checklist, including a variety of self-assessment questions, to determine teens’ internalizing behavior problems, and social and academic competencies


**Findings**

*  “**Adolescents who engaged heavily in SNS scored significantly lower in offline Activities and Academic performance** compared with adolescents who used SNS moderately”   
* Furthermore, “differences between moderate and heavier users are … larger for the younger age group,” suggesting that **this negative effect of social media is more pronounced when individuals are exposed to it at younger ages**  
* At the same time, “social competence scores appeared not to differ between the two SNS use groups,” and older adolescents actually exhibited slightly higher scores among high-SNS users than moderate SNS-users.   
    * \*\*Basically shows that social media is not all evil–just important to regulate age and be transparent about risks. Also, may have changed since 2014 lol so this is interesting to look into.   
* “**Heavier SNS users scored higher than moderate users on all three internalizing syndrome scales** and on the consolidated Internalizing scale,” and “the **negative effect** of heavier use was **more pronounced in the younger age group**”
