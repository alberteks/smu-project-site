**A Systematic Review of Social Media Use and Adolescent Identity Development** (URL: [https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40894-024-00251-1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40894-024-00251-1#Sec3)) 

*(Published Nov 2024\)*

* Researchers analyzed variety of research on different activities involved in SMU & multiple dimensions of identity development (“identity exploration and commitment, self-concept clarity, and identity distress”)   
* Sample of studies contained around 19600 adolescents w/ mean age of 16.43 y.o. (range 8-26)  
* The authors define that “**identity exploration involves a deep assessment of current commitments and questioning them to either revise or replace them with new ones**”   
  * Argue that analysis of current commitments is beneficial for identity maintenance & security, but reconsideration/exploration of different identities can sometimes lead to identity uncertainty and/or distress (both of which have been linked with depressive/anxiety symptoms, as well as “internalizing and externalizing problems”  
  * Describe that “”**process of identity development … encompasses identity exploration and commitment and ideally results in a stable self-concept, with identity distress as the less favorable outcome**”   
* Excluded studies from review that: selected participants having a clinical diagnosis, examined platforms “initially developed purely as messenger services,” included general internet use and not just SMU, and those that focused on specific social identities rather than overall identity development   
* Identity commitment & exploration were measured most frequently w/ the Utrecht Management of Identity Commitments Scale, self-concept clarity was measured most frequently with the Self-Concept Clarity Scale, and all studies which analyzed identity distress employed the Identity Distress Survey   
  * Note that identity commitment, exploration, and self-concept clarity were measured with multiple tools across studies 

* Studies found mixed results on the relationship between time spent on SM and identity exploration outcomes, but did reliably conclude that “**social media use seems to be important for identity exploration among those who actively participate**”   
  * Suggests that the **type of SM used may be a more important factor in** regulating the **relationship between SM and identity**, **than** the **amount of SMU**   
* A couple studies found that “ability and opinion comparison” activities on SM “may be associated with identity confusion, which is also beneficial for engaging in further exploration and making new commitments”   
* Many studies found very **mixed results on “the relationship between time spent on social media and self-concept clarity”**  
* Based on studies comparing the way adolescents presented themselves online and their self-concept clarity, authors concluded that “when individuals present themselves in a manner that aligns with their authentic selves, it fosters self-concept clarity”  
  * That being said, “it might also be the case that those with clearer self-concept are more confident to be who they actually are on social media”  
    * Seems very likely to me so initial point/authors’ conclusion should be taken w/ grain of salt   
* For **identity distress**, **studies were** once again **mixed**, **but** one **potential relationship was ability comparison activities** on social media **correlating to identity distress**  
    
* Authors’ primary conclusion was that “**it is *not* the amount of time spent on social media that is critical, but rather the activities undertaken during this time that seem to play a role for aspects of identity development**”
